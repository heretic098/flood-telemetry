Hydrological Physics Engine & Telemetry Methodology

This document outlines the real-time data sources, hydraulic physics equations, data provenance confidence tiers, and operational rules governing the Somerset Levels & Moors Flood Monitoring Dashboard.

🌐 1. Open Data Feeds & Server Architecture

The application ingests high-frequency 15-minute telemetry directly from the UK Environment Agency (EA) open API:

📡 EA Flood Monitoring API

Fetches live stage elevations in meters above Ordnance Datum Newlyn (mAOD) across 8 critical gauging stations.

https://environment.data.gov.uk/flood-monitoring/id/measures/{id}/readings

⚡ SQLite Embedded WAL Cache

A background process (server.js) syncs all 8 station measures every 5 minutes into a local SQLite database (telemetry.db in WAL mode). The frontend fetches /api/status for instant < 20ms page loads without waiting for external API calls.

🏷️ 2. Data Provenance & Visual Confidence Badges

To ensure complete scientific integrity, every infrastructure card displays an explicit data provenance badge indicating whether its status is directly measured, physically enforced, or inferred from operational models:

📡 DEDICATED SENSORS HIGH CONFIDENCE

Derived directly from paired physical sensors deployed at the structure (e.g. Monk's Leaze Upstream 52233 vs Downstream Sowy 52234).

⚖️ HYDROSTATIC LAW HIGH CONFIDENCE

Governed by absolute head differential pressure laws (e.g., Dunball Tidal Outfall flap doors where seawater stage exceeding inland drain stage physically forces passive doors shut).

⚡ LEVEL SOP MODEL MEDIUM CONFIDENCE

Inferred from official EA Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) level triggers because public EA telemetry does not expose real-time SCADA electrical pump status.

📐 2B. 4-Tier Threshold Provenance Taxonomy

In accordance with our core principles of Epistemic Humility ([RULE-HUM-01]) and Empirical Substantiation ([RULE-SUB-01]), every hydraulic elevation, weir crest, alert stage, and pumping trigger on this platform is strictly categorized into one of four auditable provenance tiers:

📐 TIER 1: SURVEYED GROUND TRUTH

Fixed physical civil geometry in mAOD surveyed relative to Ordnance Datum Newlyn (ODN). Includes concrete weir crests (Hookbridge 7.45m), clyse sills (Monk's Leaze 4.20m), levee defense crests (Baltmoor Wall 7.80m), and road crowns (Cuts Road 7.35m).

📋 TIER 2: AGENCY PLAN STATUTORY TRIGGER

Official operational action levels published in statutory civil contingency documents, specifically the EA Somerset Levels & Moors Trigger Point Plan (e.g. Currymoor PS mobile pumps ordered at 5.80m, active at 6.10m; Northmoor PS mobile ordered at 4.80m, active at 5.20m).

📊 TIER 3: EA TYPICAL STATISTICAL ENVELOPE

In-channel river station alert levels calibrated directly against the Environment Agency open telemetry API stageScale.typicalRangeHigh historical envelope (e.g., Greenham 76.99m, Bishops Hull 17.48m, Taunton 14.24m).

⚙️ TIER 4: DERIVED PLATFORM HEURISTIC

Empirical software interpretation rules and safety buffers applied to raw stage measurements (e.g. 15cm Near Crest pre-warning buffer or hydrostatic gate differential checks Δh). Never asserts simulated SCADA or unmeasured cumecs.

📖 Full evidence register and station-by-station citations are documented in the repository under docs/threshold_provenance.md ↗.

📐 3. Hydrological & Hydraulic Core Equations

A. Spillway Overtopping & Submergence (Villemonte Weir Model)

Given upstream river stage $h_{\text{river}}$, moor basin stage $h_{\text{moor}}$, and surveyed crest elevation $Z_{\text{crest}}$:

$$H_u = h_{\text{river}} - Z_{\text{crest}} \quad \text{and} \quad H_d = h_{\text{moor}} - Z_{\text{crest}}$$ $$\text{Submergence Ratio } S = \frac{H_d}{H_u}$$
  • SECURE: $H_u < -0.15\text{m}$ (River level is >15cm below spillway crest).
  • NEAR CREST: $-0.15\text{m} \le H_u < 0\text{m}$ (River level is within 15cm of overtopping).
  • FREE OVERTOPPING: $H_u \ge 0\text{m}$ and $S < 0.70$ (Water flows freely over spillway crest into moor).
  • DROWNED / SUBMERGED: $H_u \ge 0\text{m}$ and $S \ge 0.70$ (High tailwater in flooded moor drowns the spillway).
  • REVERSE SPILL: $H_d > H_u \ge 0\text{m}$ (Flooded moor level exceeds river level, spilling back into channel).

Applied across all engineered weirs and inter-moor cascades: Hookbridge Spillway ($Z_{\text{crest}} = 7.45\text{m AOD}$, Tone ➔ Curry Moor), Athelney Spillway & Cuts Road ($Z_{\text{crest}} = 7.35\text{m AOD}$, Curry Moor ➔ Northmoor cascade), and Baltmoor Wall Defense ($Z_{\text{crest}} = 7.80\text{m AOD}$, Parrett confluence levee defense).

B. Submerged Gate Volumetric Discharge Equation

Discharge across Monk's Leaze Clyse into the Sowy Relief Channel is estimated using the hydraulic submerged gate formula:

$$Q = C_d \cdot B \cdot H_{\text{head}} \cdot \sqrt{2g \Delta h}$$

Where $C_d = 0.60$ (discharge coefficient), $B = 4.20\text{m}$ (gate width), $H_{\text{head}} = h_{\text{up}} - Z_{\text{threshold}}$ (Diversion Threshold $Z_{\text{threshold}} = 4.20\text{m}$ AOD; Structure Sill / Wall Height $Z_{\text{structure}} = 6.70\text{m}$ AOD), and $\Delta h = h_{\text{up}} - h_{\text{down}}$.

📡 5. Dynamic Crest History & Future Remote Sensing Innovations

A. Time-Versioned Crest Elevation Engine (crest_history)

Spillway crest elevations ($Z_{\text{crest}}$) are evaluated dynamically using date-stamped configuration records in hydro_config.json. When inspecting historical archives or replaying multi-year flood events, getActiveCrestHeight() matches the telemetry timestamp against effective survey date windows (e.g., pre- vs. post-2014 Baltmoor Wall raising or SRA capacity improvement schemes).

B. Future Roadmap: Automated Remote Sensing (DEFRA LiDAR & InSAR)

To move beyond static SOP manuals, future roadmap iterations will integrate automated external validation pipelines:

  • DEFRA 25cm / 1m LiDAR DTM API: Programmatically sampling ground elevation rasters along spillway vector transects across survey years (1998–present) to derive true minimum crest elevations ($Z_{\text{crest}}$).
  • Copernicus Sentinel-1 InSAR (EGMS): Utilizing Interferometric Synthetic Aperture Radar time-series to monitor millimeter-scale embankment settling and track post-construction bank height modifications continuously.

🚗 6. Community Roads & Highways Passability Methodology

Somerset Levels highways are vital community lifelines, not open-channel spillways. Because road closures are governed by both physical water height and Somerset Council Highways emergency gate procedures, this platform evaluates road passability through an empirical Multi-Factor Passability Model.

A. The 4-Factor Passability Evaluation Model

Rather than relying on a single isolated sensor, road access status is evaluated across four empirical inputs:

1. WATER STAGE (mAOD) ELEVATION

Live water height relative to Ordnance Datum Newlyn (mAOD) compared directly against surveyed road crowns or roadside ditch bankfull elevations.

2. 3-HOUR MOMENTUM (dh/dt) RATE OF CHANGE

Moving rate-of-rise over 3 hours. A rate ≥ +0.03m/h (3cm/h) into low-lying moors indicates massive flood inflow, triggering rapid-surge closure advisories.

3. STATUTORY EA WARNINGS AGENCY DATA

Live ingestion of official Environment Agency Flood Alerts (Amber) and Flood Warnings (Red) across 5 statutory target areas covering our road network.

4. COUNCIL GATE PROTOCOLS PHYSICAL SAFETY

Somerset Council Highways emergency closure procedures, daylight operating windows (08:00–17:00), and post-flood road safety inspections.

C. The Post-Flood Reopening Protocol & Daylight Inspection Window

A frequent resident question is: "Why does the dashboard show 'Clearing / Inspecting' when water has already drained off the road?"

Somerset Council emergency road closures are enforced by heavy steel gates secured with padlocks. When floodwaters recede below asphalt elevation, the highway is never declared open immediately:

  • Physical Hazard Window: Receding river water frequently deposits inches of thick, slippery river silt, submerged debris, or washouts on road verges. Additionally, hydrostatic pressure can undermine roadbed foundations (scour).
  • Daylight Operating Window: Somerset Council Highways inspection crews inspect structures and unlock gates during daylight operating hours (08:00 to 17:00 GMT). Gates will not be reopened in darkness before a visual safety inspection has been conducted.
  • The 4-Stage Reopening Gate: A corridor only transitions from ENCROACHING (CLEARING) to PASSABLE when:
    1. The road crown has remained continuously dry ($h < Z_{\text{crown}} - 3\text{cm}$) without re-submergence.
    2. Roadside ditch drainage has dropped below caution deadband ($h < Z_{\text{caution}} - 5\text{cm}$).
    3. The Environment Agency has officially deactivated the Flood Warning for that target area.
    4. EITHER 6 daylight hours (08:00–17:00) have elapsed since drainage, OR a 24-hour total dry safety timeout is reached.

D. Epistemic Humility & Community Ground-Truth Grounding

In accordance with [RULE-HUM-01] and [RULE-PROV-01], this platform upholds strict scientific modesty:

No Simulated Padlock Sensors: We do not claim to possess automated electronic telemetry on Somerset Council's padlocks. Gate status is synthesized strictly from published council flood contingency plans and empirical water stages.

Optical Verification via Live Community Webcams: To empower residents with independent ground truth, the dashboard provides direct links to live 3-minute cameras at the Athelney Spillway road crossing and Huish Episcopi. Drivers are encouraged to visually check the water line, road carriageway, and spillway crest overtopping before traveling.

📜 7. Public Safety, Highways & Open Source Licensing

🛑 Life-Safety & Emergency Non-Displacement Notice

Information displayed across this dashboard and topology map is synthesized from third-party public telemetry and hydraulic models. This platform does not provide official flood warnings, emergency evacuation instructions, or life-safety guidance.

For official UK Government flood warnings and statutory emergency instructions, always consult the GOV.UK Flood Warning Service ↗ or telephone the 24-hour Environment Agency Floodline on 0345 988 1188. In an immediate life-threatening emergency, always dial 999.

🚗 Community Roads, Access & Emergency Gate Disclaimer

Road passability assessments (such as PASSABLE, ENCROACHING, or IMPASSABLE on Cuts Road, Muchelney access roads, or the A361) are theoretical mathematical estimates derived from river and moor stage sensors relative to surveyed road crown elevations (mAOD).

  • No Ground Inspection: This platform cannot observe local ditch blockages, localized flash ponding, structural road washouts, silt accumulation, or surface ice.
  • Physical Flood Gates: Emergency road flood gates (including Somerset Council gates on the A361 at East Lyng and Burrowbridge, and at Cuts Road) are operated independently by Somerset Council Highways and emergency contractors. Gates may be closed, locked, or manned at any time regardless of telemetry readings.
  • No Reliance / Highway Code Compliance: NEVER attempt to drive through floodwater regardless of displayed estimates. As little as 30cm (12 inches) of moving water can float a family car. You remain solely responsible for your vehicle and safety under the UK Highway Code.
  • Official Travel Authority: For official road closures, roadworks, and travel alerts, visit Somerset Council's official service at Travel Somerset ↗ or follow @TravelSomerset ↗.
